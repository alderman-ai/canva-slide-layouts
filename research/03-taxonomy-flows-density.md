## (a) Archetypes to add (candidates beyond a typical 62)

Structural / meta:
1. **Section Title + Description** (structural) — section header with a side paragraph; 1 title, 1 para. [Google Slides]
2. **Topic Agenda / Progress Tracker** (structural) — agenda re-shown with current item highlighted; 3–8 items. [Keynote Topic Agenda; think-cell agenda]
3. **Contact / Thank-you** (structural) — name, channels, QR; 1–5 fields. [Beautiful.ai]
4. **Divider with Question** (structural) — SCQA "Question" slide; 1 line. [Analyst Academy]
5. **Executive Summary 3-column** (text) — Situation | Findings | Recommendation, 4–5 lines per column. [StrategyU]
6. **Decisions Needed / The Ask** (text) — explicit asks, 1–4 items. [Deckary status; Waveup]
7. **Definition** (text) — term + definition; 1–2. [Keynote]
8. **Caption-only / Full-bleed with lower-third** (image) — 1 image, 1 caption. [Google Slides CAPTION_ONLY]

Numbers / data:
9. **Chart + Insight Callout** (numbers) — chart 2/3 + so-what box 1/3, arrow to datum; 1 chart, 1–3 callouts. [StrategyU]
10. **Metric Dashboard tiles** (numbers) — 4–6 tiles with trend/sparkline. [StrategyU]
11. **Waterfall / Bridge** (numbers) — 3–8 bars. [think-cell]
12. **Marimekko** (numbers) — 3–8 columns × 2–6 segments. [think-cell]
13. **Thermometer / Progress-to-goal** (numbers) — 1 metric, 1 target. [Beautiful.ai]
14. **Pictograph / Unit chart** (numbers) — 1–4 series. [Beautiful.ai]
15. **Scatter / XY plot** (numbers) — 5–50 points. [Beautiful.ai; Zelazny correlation]
16. **Data table w/ status glyphs** (numbers) — RAG dots or checkboxes; 3–7 rows. [Deckary]
17. **ROI / calculator inputs** (numbers) — 2–5 variables. [Beautiful.ai]

Comparison / evaluation:
18. **Harvey Ball Scorecard** (comparison) — 3–4 options × 6–7 criteria. [StrategyU]
19. **Quote + Evidence** (comparison) — 1 quote + 1 stat/chart. [StrategyU]
20. **Pricing tiers** (comparison) — 2–4 columns. [Pitch]
21. **Venn** (comparison) — 2–4 sets. [Beautiful.ai; Infodiagram]
22. **Double list w/ central visual** (comparison) — 2×2–5 items. [Infodiagram]

Frameworks / structure:
23. **Issue / Hypothesis / Driver tree** (frameworks) — 1 root, 2–4 branches, 2–4 leaves each (≤4 per level per Kosslyn). [Slideworks; Kosslyn]
24. **Pillar** (frameworks) — 2–5 pillars on a base. [Infodiagram]
25. **Onion / concentric** (frameworks) — 3–5 rings. [Infodiagram]
26. **Iceberg** (frameworks) — 1–3 visible, 2–5 hidden. [Infodiagram]
27. **Layer / Stack** (frameworks) — 3–6 layers. [Duarte Stack]
28. **Target / Bullseye** (frameworks) — 3–4 rings. [Beautiful.ai]
29. **Hub & Spoke / Radiate** (frameworks) — 1 hub, 3–8 spokes. [Duarte Network]
30. **Segment (pie-of-concepts)** (frameworks) — 3–6 wedges. [Duarte Segment]
31. **Join / Convergence** (frameworks) — 2–4 inputs → 1 output. [Duarte Join]
32. **Word cloud** (text/frameworks) — 15–40 terms. [Beautiful.ai]

Process / time:
33. **Chevron sequence** (process) — 3–6. [Infodiagram]
34. **Stair / maturity** (process) — 3–5 steps. [Infodiagram]
35. **Timeline 3-layer** (process) — phases above, milestones on line, risks below; 3–8 milestones. [StrategyU]
36. **Gantt** (process) — 4–12 rows, 1 timescale. [think-cell]
37. **Kanban / status board** (process) — 3–4 columns × 2–5 cards. [Beautiful.ai]
38. **Calendar** (process) — 1 month grid. [Beautiful.ai]
39. **Customer journey map** (process) — 4–7 stages × 2–4 rows. [Beautiful.ai]
40. **Flowchart w/ decisions** (process) — 4–10 nodes. [Beautiful.ai]
41. **Divergent / convergent flow** (process) — 1→2–4 or 2–4→1. [slide:ology]
42. **Countdown / timer** (process, workshop-only) — 1 value. [Beautiful.ai]

Image / people / product:
43. **Device frame (laptop / desktop / phone)** (image) — 1–3 screens. [Pitch device frames; Beautiful.ai]
44. **Logo grid / wall** (image) — 6–24 logos. [Beautiful.ai]
45. **Org chart** (people) — 1 root, 2–5 per level, ≤3 levels. [Beautiful.ai]
46. **Map with markers** (image/numbers) — 1 map, 1–10 markers. [Beautiful.ai]
47. **Photo 3-up / gallery** (image) — 3–6 images. [Keynote]
48. **Icon + label row ("Three Things")** (text) — exactly 3 (2–4). [StrategyU]
49. **Web/embed view** (image) — 1 frame. [Beautiful.ai Webview; Gamma embed]
50. **Full video** (image) — 1. [Beautiful.ai]

## (b) Flow templates (ordered archetype sequences)

1. **SCQA → Pyramid consulting deck** (20–60 slides, 30–60 min or read-deck): Title → Exec Summary (SCR; Resolution = 60–70% of text) → Situation (context slide, nothing controversial) → Complication → Question/Agenda → per pillar: Section header → 3–6 Action-title + evidence slides (chart+callout, Harvey ball, table, tree) → Recommendation/Next steps (Gantt or roadmap) → Appendix. Titles alone must carry the argument ("title test"). Sources: [Supernormal on McKinsey](https://www.supernormal.com/blog/mckinsey-presentation-playbook), [Analyst Academy](https://www.theanalystacademy.com/powerpoint-storytelling/), [Slideworks](https://slideworks.io/resources/how-mckinsey-consultants-make-presentations), [Deckary exec summary](https://deckary.com/blog/executive-summary-slides).
2. **Sequoia pitch** (10 core, 12–15 with adds; 20 min): Title → Company purpose (one-liner statement) → Problem (1–4 bullets) → Solution → Why now (timeline) → Market size (big number/TAM stack) → Competition (2×2 or Harvey ball) → Product (device frames) → Business model (flow/table) → Team (people grid) → Financials (chart/table) → [+Traction chart after Solution if strong; +GTM; +Ask/use of funds]. Sources: [Slidebean Sequoia](https://slidebean.com/templates/sequoia-pitch-deck-template), [Waveup](https://waveup.com/blog/how-to-supercharge-sequoia-pitch-deck-template/).
3. **Kawasaki 10/20/30** (10 slides, 20 min, ≥30pt): Title → Problem/Opportunity → Value proposition → Underlying magic (diagram/demo) → Business model → Go-to-market → Competitive analysis → Management team → Financial projections & key metrics → Current status/timeline/use of funds. Sources: [Kawasaki](https://guykawasaki.com/the_102030_rule/), [SlideModel](https://slidemodel.com/the-10-20-30-rule-of-presentations/).
4. **Slidebean 3-act pitch** (15–22 slides): Act 1 Cover (5–7-word tagline) → Traction teaser → Problem → Market overview; Act 2 Solution → Product (1–2) → How it works (steps) → Target audience → Case study → Business model → Roadmap; Act 3 Traction → Unit economics → GTM (2–3 channels) → TAM → Competition → Secret sauce → Team → Financials → Ask. Source: [Slidebean](https://slidebean.com/blog/pitch-deck-slides-a-complete-guide).
5. **Duarte Sparkline (keynote/talk)** (18–40 slides, 18–45 min, 1–3 slides/min): Title → "What is" (statement/photo) → Call to adventure (gap: big number or contrast) → alternating What is / What could be pairs ×3–5 (before/after, quote, photo, single stat) → Call to action (statement) → New bliss (full-bleed image). Sources: [Duarte storytelling](https://www.duarte.com/resources/storytelling-framework/), [JD Schramm](https://jdschramm.substack.com/p/embracing-the-sparkline).
6. **Teaching / Gagné 9 events** (per module ~10–20 slides; 1 slide per 1–2 min): Hook (stat/image/question) → Objectives (3–4 bullets) → Recall prompt → Concept (definition + diagram) → Worked example → Guided case → Practice/activity instructions → Feedback/answer reveal → Quiz/check → Recap + action plan/transfer. Sources: [Symonds Research](https://symondsresearch.com/gagnes-9-events-of-instruction/), [Structural Learning](https://www.structural-learning.com/post/gagnes-nine-events-of-instruction-teachers-guide).
7. **Status update** (4–6 slides, ≤30 s exec scan per slide): Header + overall RAG → Multi-dimension RAG table (schedule/budget/scope/resources/quality; ≤5–7 workstreams) → Milestones/timeline → Accomplishments (2–4) → Upcoming (2–4) → Risks & issues (2–4, likelihood×impact) → Decisions needed. Sources: [Deckary](https://deckary.com/blog/project-status-slide), [Analysistabs](https://analysistabs.com/templates/project-status-report/).
8. **Workshop** (15–30 slides per 2 h; activity every 15–20 min): Welcome → Objectives → Agenda w/ times → Icebreaker → per block: Framing content (≤3 slides) → Activity instructions (steps, materials, time) → Timer → Debrief prompts → Parking lot → Key takeaways → Next steps/feedback. Source: [SlideModel](https://slidemodel.com/workshop-presentation/), [SlideUplift](https://slideuplift.com/powerpoint-templates/workshop-facilitation-deck/).
9. **Executive-summary-first read-deck (slidedoc)** (10–30 pages, unpresented): Cover → 1-page exec summary → Contents → per topic: Section title+description → 2–4 dense text+figure pages (100–250 words) → Recommendations → Appendix. Sources: [Duarte slidedocs](https://www.duarte.com/resources/guides-tools/slidedocs-visual-documents/), [Pallotta on Duarte](https://www.lucapallotta.com/slidedocs/).
10. **Lessig/Takahashi rapid talk** (60–200 slides, 3–5 slides/min): Title → one word/phrase/image per slide, synchronized to speech; no bullets. Sources: [Wikipedia Takahashi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Takahashi_method), [Ethos3 Lessig](https://ethos3.com/designstyles-and-approachespresenting-lessig-way/).

## (c) Density rubric (levels 1–5)

| Level | Name | Info units / slide | Text budget | Pace | Grounding |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Cinematic | 1 idea; 1 visual + ≤1 line | ≤6 words (TED), ≤2 lines, ≥42pt | 2–5 slides/min | TED "6 words/slide", "1–2 lines will do", one idea per slide ([Moxie](https://www.moxieinstitute.com/how-to-make-good-presentation-slides-ted-style/), [TEDx guide](https://www.ted.com/participate/organize-a-local-tedx-event/tedx-organizer-guide/speakers-program/prepare-your-speaker/create-prepare-slides)); Takahashi/Lessig |
| 2 | Talk | 1 idea; ≤3 chunks | ≤6 lines total; ≤25 words; ≥30pt | 1–2 slides/min | TEDx "max six lines"; Kawasaki 30pt / 2 min per slide ([Kawasaki](https://guykawasaki.com/the_102030_rule/)); glance test 3 s ([HBR/Duarte](https://hbr.org/2012/10/do-your-slides-pass-the-glance-test)) |
| 3 | Briefing | 1 message; ≤4 bullets × ≤4 units (nouns+verbs), ≤2 lines each; ≤4 items per tree level | ≈40–60 words; ≥20pt body | 1 slide/1–2 min | Kosslyn "rule of four", 20pt min ([Kosslyn et al. 2012](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/psychology/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2012.00230/full), [UCSD](https://multimedia.ucsd.edu/best-practices/presentation-design.html)); Cowan ~4 chunks ([Cowan 2001](https://philpapers.org/rec/COWTMN)); 6×6 rule = 36-word ceiling ([WPS](https://www.wps.com/blog/6x6-powerpoint-rule-what-is-it-and-how-to-use-it/)) |
| 4 | Consulting | 1 action title (≤15 words / ≤8 words McKinsey, ≤2 lines) + 2–4 supporting points; exec summary 3 cols × 4–5 lines; dashboard 4–6 tiles; Harvey 3–4 × 6–7; process ≤4–5 steps | ≈60–100 words; body 14–16pt | 60 s/slide presented, or read | [Deckary](https://deckary.com/blog/consulting-slide-standards), [Slideworks McKinsey](https://slideworks.io/resources/how-mckinsey-consultants-make-presentations), [Slideworks BCG](https://slideworks.io/resources/bcg-approach-to-great-slides-practical-guide-from-former-consultant), [StrategyU](https://strategyu.co/slide-layouts/) |
| 5 | Slidedoc | multi-paragraph; several figures | 100 words target, 250 max; >250 → write a document | not presented | Duarte slidedocs via [Pallotta](https://www.lucapallotta.com/slidedocs/), [FlowVella](https://flowvella.com/blogslidedocs-a-new-concept-from-nancy-duarte/) |

Pace norms by deck type: pitch 10–15 slides/10–20 min; conference 1/min; lecture 20–45 per 45–90 min; workshop 40–80 per 1–4 h; webinar 30–55 per 30–60 min ([SlideEgg](https://www.slideegg.com/blog/presentation-tips/how-many-slides-should-a-presentation-have-by-use-case/), [Envato](https://business.tutsplus.com/tutorials/how-many-slides-use-presentation--cms-34652), [Lethbridge](https://www.ulethbridge.ca/teachingcentre/creating-lesson-lecture-or-presentation)). Mayer's redundancy/coherence/signaling/segmenting principles justify levels 1–3 for spoken delivery and forbid verbatim narration of on-screen text ([Devlin Peck](https://www.devlinpeck.com/content/mayers-principles-of-multimedia-learning)).

Script rule: `level = f(mode)`: live talk → ≤2; briefing/teaching → 3; consulting/board → 4; read-deck → 5. Overflow triggers a split (segmenting) before density is raised.

## (d) Polish-cost rubric

Anchor: 24Slides tiers — fix-up $16, redesign $43, redraw (custom data viz, illustration, diagram, animation) $66 per slide ([PitchWorx](https://pitchworx.com/how-much-does-presentation-design-cost)); data-viz/infographic slides cost 30–60% more, custom illustration +20–50% ([Whitepage](https://www.whitepage.studio/blog/presentation-design-cost), [SlideGenius](https://www.slidegenius.com/cm-faq-question/how-much-does-a-presentation-designer-typically-charge-for-their-services)). Map to 1–5:

| Cost | Definition | Family defaults |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Typography only; looks good with type scale + spacing | Structural (title, section, agenda, statement, big number, quote), text (bullets, two-column text, definition) |
| 2 | Typography + primitives (icons, rules, cards, RAG dots) | Comparison tables, icon rows, pricing, Harvey ball, exec summary, simple chevrons |
| 3 | Needs a real chart engine or supplied photo; alignment-sensitive | Numbers (bar/line/pie/waterfall/scatter), timeline, Gantt, image+text, team grid, logo grid |
| 4 | Needs curated/high-res imagery or a multi-element diagram | Full-bleed photo, photo 3-up, device mockups, maps, org chart, trees, journey map, Marimekko, dashboards |
| 5 | Bespoke illustration/infographic, animation or video, live embed | Infographic, pictograph, word cloud, full video, webview, ROI calculator, iceberg/onion metaphors |

Rationale: text-only slides are "considerably" cheaper; cost climbs with dependence on external assets (photos), rendering engines (charts), and hand-drawn composition (diagrams/infographics). Kosslyn's compatibility principle also means a wrong chart form is a visible defect, so numbers family carries higher failure risk than text.

## Notes and gaps

- Beautiful.ai's "300+" figure counts variants; the public named set is the 62 above.
- Pitch, Canva, and Figma kits publish categories, not exhaustive layout names (Figma pages are 403 to fetchers; Canva's help only says "browse suggested layouts" ([Canva](https://www.canva.com/help/using-layouts/))).
- The 6×6/7×7 rules have no research origin; the Kosslyn/Cowan 4-item limit and Mayer's principles are the defensible basis for level 3.
